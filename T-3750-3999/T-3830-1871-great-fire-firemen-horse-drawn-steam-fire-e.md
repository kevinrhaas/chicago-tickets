Research: `docs/RESEARCH/fire-night-scenes-3814/README.md`, section "Firemen, engines and water". In short (Andreas, *History of Chicago* vol. 2):

- The department had "seventeen steam engines, fifty-four hose carts, four hook and ladder trucks, two hose elevators, one fire-escape, eleven alarm bells, and forty-eight thousand feet of hose" (p. 702). Steamers were horse-drawn; firemen rode.
- At the barn: the "Little Giant" No. 6 was "in position at the corner of DeKoven and Jefferson streets" within two and a half minutes of a still alarm, laid a line "through Patrick O'Leary's lot", and "for more than thirty minutes this engine battled alone"; the stream was so strong "one man could not hold the pipe", and the men "were obliged to call for such aid as could be obtained, in carrying and holding their hose and pipes" (p. 711). The "Chicago" No. 5 took the plug at Forquer and Taylor, broke down, and its men "tore up the sidewalk and took boards from the fence near by to use as quick fuel" until the coal ran out (p. 712).
- The engines "had all been working on the West Side; and they could not reel-up six hundred feet of hose each, and cross the river" in time (p. 710). "After the Water Works burned, the firemen could do little good with their engines, except on the banks of the river": 7,500 ft of hose and one engine lost, fourteen engines left after eleven on Sunday night, half the hose by daybreak (p. 710). Firemen came by rail from "nearly every city, far and near" with their apparatus (p. 710).
- Saturday night's fire (the jaunt's earlier ground): "A dozen leads of hose ... the pipemen stood their ground within a rod of the fiery tempest" (p. 705); citizens with "portable fire-extinguishers ... directed their miniature engines" on a saloon's walls (p. 706).
- Water without the mains: men with buckets on roofs (pp. 742-743, T-3817), the store's "own pumping engines" at Field, Leiter (pp. 734-735), water carts on Monday (p. 753).
