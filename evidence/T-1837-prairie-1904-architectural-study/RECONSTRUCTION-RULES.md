# Working design rules for undocumented architecture

These are **proposed reconstruction priors**, not measurements of Prairie Avenue houses. Use them only after reading the property's full-resolution maps and photographs. A reliable property-specific dimension, photo silhouette or surviving drawing overrides a prior. Do not randomly select a style: the register's construction family and the property's dated neighbours constrain the choice.

## Turn a mapped polygon into a buildable exterior

1. Trace the main body, each attached wing, porch/bay and each detached rear polygon as separate entities. Record sheet, source pixel polygon, scale control, geographic transform and positional uncertainty. Do not treat the viewer's parcel rectangle as the house footprint. Keep party walls and the public sidewalk fixed while reconciling mismatches.
2. Record the raw map story/material symbols and their interpretation from the relevant key. Where the photo and notation disagree, retain both readings and decide whether basement, attic or a stepped rear wing explains the difference. Never use an unresolved symbol as an exact elevation measurement.
3. Set grade, basement sill, principal floor, successive floors, cornice, eave, ridge and chimney datums explicitly. Tie the entry stair to these datums and the actual walk grade. Establish the main roof before placing ornamental front gables or dormers.
4. Declare the source basis for each facade independently. For an unseen rear, use restrained service construction: regular sash aligned to floors, lintels, one service entrance and only the circulation/stable openings supported by the plan or a documented analogue. Do not mirror the street facade onto the alley.
5. Make the working design internally plausible: wall piers support lintels/arches; bays have foundations or corbels; gutters lead to downpipes; chimneys pierce roofs with flashing; doors clear landings; gates align with entries; party-wall windows do not open into a neighbour's wall.
6. Publish the chosen values and bounded alternatives in the source sidecar. The facade is permitted to look complete while its invented details remain explicitly labelled reconstructed.

## Starting ranges, subject to source control

| Parameter | Proposed starting range or rule | Application / guard |
|---|---|---|
| Frame-house principal storey | 2.8–3.5 m floor-to-floor | Small older villas; obtain eave height from a scaled view before committing |
| Masonry-house principal storey | 3.2–4.2 m; upper storeys often 2.9–3.7 m | Townhouses and mansions; grand halls may exceed this, and are not forced into the range |
| Raised principal floor | 0.8–1.8 m above local grade | Derive actual steps and area wells from photographs; a mapped basement does not prove this rise |
| Front-window layout | Begin with 2–3 principal bays on a narrow rowhouse, 3–5 on a detached villa | An observed porch, tower or irregular footprint can replace this grid; counts remain conjectural |
| Sash proportions | Typical clear opening approximately 0.75–1.15 m wide and 1.5–2.4 m high | Use only for an undocumented ordinary sash; grouped windows and landmark arches are individually authored |
| Main roof | Ordinary hip/gable working pitch 25–45°; steep Gothic/Chateau work 45–65° | Street silhouette outranks the range; retain mansard lower and upper planes separately |
| Mansard | Lower face working pitch 65–80°, upper roof 10–30° | Not proof of a specific profile; photograph/drawing determines straight versus curved lower face |
| Stone/timber entry stair | Working riser 0.14–0.19 m, tread 0.25–0.34 m | Solve integer risers from floor height and visible run; do not apply modern code as historical evidence |
| Solid exterior wall / reveal | Start masonry envelope thickness 0.23–0.45 m and frame 0.12–0.22 m | Structural family and opening depth only; the unseen wall assembly is reconstructed |
| Clapboard | Start exposure 0.10–0.16 m, with a shallow physical lap | Match visible board count when a photo permits; no giant plank texture |
| Brickwork | Begin near an 0.20 × 0.06 m visible brick face plus 0.006–0.012 m joint | Historic local products varied; calibrate to doors, known courses and source specification |
| Slate courses | Begin near 0.20–0.30 m width and 0.15–0.25 m exposure | Slate format/pattern is house-specific; avoid scaling one roof texture with every building |
| Rear service building | Respect mapped one/two-storey and mixed-material subdivisions | Carriage aperture, loft and ramp must fit the owner's service route; no universal shed size |

All metric values are modelling priors. Preserve historical dimensions in their original units and convert once. Record which prior controlled the result, which visual checks supported it and what new evidence would replace it. Do not assign these values to the attested or measured confidence tier.

## Family-specific composition rules

| Family | Required recognizable construction | What can be reconstructed conservatively |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier frame / Italianate villa | Clapboard or supported cladding, tall openings, bracketed eaves, timber porch and small-scale massing | Unseen service windows, restrained board colour, rear stair and roof drainage |
| Stone-front urban house | Street ashlar facing, brick returns, high stoop, genuine sash recesses, bounded cornice and area well | Hidden masonry courses, understated rear trim and window subdivisions |
| Second Empire | Distinct mansard and dormer rhythm, deep cornice, bay/porch hierarchy and tall stacks | Rear dormer placement only within mapped/visible roof constraints; no invented monumental tower |
| Gothic / Queen Anne | Property-specific pointed/shaped gable, tower/oriel relationship, steep roof intersections and contrasting trim | Secondary timber or masonry patterns using local analogues without copying a named neighbour |
| Romanesque | Thick round-arched reveals, convincing voussoirs, stone relief, heavy base and supported bay/oriel | Original restrained foliate patterns where carving is illegible; record that they are newly designed |
| Georgian / classical | Ordered bays, flat/dressed trim, coherent cornice, door/fanlight and dated parapet or roof | Sash subdivisions and modest rear service treatment, without copying later restorations |

## Per-building completion checklist

Each house needs: footprint and grade; foundation/basement; street, side and rear wall assemblies; roof planes and coverings; dormers/skylights only where supported; chimneys and flashings; genuine window openings, sash, glass and enclosed visual backing; doors and transoms; entry landings/stoops; attached porches/bays/towers; cornice/parapet/cresting; rainwater goods; service connection; boundary and ground contact; source/confidence sidecar; full/balanced/light outputs; fixed-camera evidence. Detached coach houses and glasshouses remain separate records even when batched for rendering.

Unresolved colour does not mean every house should be grey: choose a physically plausible material palette from the specified family, label it reconstructed, and vary weathering according to the building's age in 1904. Avoid random colour noise and modern decay. Landmark-specific carvings and silhouettes require custom geometry; shared components provide construction, not interchangeable whole facades.
