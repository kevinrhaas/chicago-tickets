Eleven South Water dwelling and lodging roofs stand with no household: four D1 cabins, two D2 shanties, two D3 and two D4 cottages and the H1 house.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)


Counted by T-1641, closing South Water's books, on the committed tree. The district's five
platted blocks carry 52 structure records. Twenty-two carry neither `occupants` nor a
`resident_assignment`. Eleven of those are ancillary — A1 stable, A2 barn or carriage shed,
A3 privy — and an outbuilding with no household of its own is the model working, not a gap.
The other eleven are roofs raised as dwellings or lodging and they stand empty:

T-1200's stop condition is "the district's slot list reads built, every roof occupied", so
on this count the district is built and not occupied. T-1638 seated nine of these blocks'
roofs through the placement policy's platted deal under its `labourer_dwellings` and
`tradesman_dwellings` clauses; these eleven were not reached by it, and WHY is the first
thing to establish — a household programme with nobody left to seat is a different finding
from a clause whose predicate these eleven fall outside of, and only one of them is fixable
by seating.

**Acceptance:** each of the eleven either carries a household with the same
`resident_assignment` shape T-1638 wrote (status, confidence, note naming the clause and the
lot, `household_id`), or is recorded — in the placement policy, not in prose — as a roof the
programme has nobody for, with the count of households actually left. No household is
invented to fill a roof: if the town's reconstructed households are exhausted, an empty
cottage on South Water is the honest answer and saying so is the deliverable. Visible either
way: eleven cards gain a household, or eleven cards say why they have none.
