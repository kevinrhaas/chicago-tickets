## Finding from T-1558, 2026-09-25 — the 265 cannot be made, and the rule yields 73

T-1558 published the cost ladder at `data/reconstruction/1835_refamily_rule.json`
(report: `docs/RESEARCH/1835_refamily_rule.md`) and named, person by person, the 1,159
reconstructed people standing in the 48 refused buckets. Its measurement bears on the
number this ticket spends:

- **The held surplus and the open orders are disjoint on every axis.** Not one of the 48
  refused buckets has a single open slot in its own (sex, age band, household kind,
  trade) class in ANY division, and not one of the 69 open buckets holds any surplus. A
  move that changes only the DIVISION — the one axis that is a bare ledger allocation for
  most held people, and so the only free one — therefore yields **0**.
- **265 was taken from the aggregate and the aggregate cannot see the axes.** A move may
  not re-sex or re-age anybody (T-1557's own fault check), and on that alone the ceiling
  is **233**: 285 of the 427 open slots stand in adult-male cells while the surplus holds
  287 people under twenty and 186 adult women.
- Holding the TRADE fixed as well — it is written on the person's own card with its own
  basis, so re-trading a man is a fresh reconstruction decision and not a re-family —
  brings the ceiling to **123**.
- Applying movability (an adoption naming a house refuses the move; a placed roof refuses
  it; a division a SOURCE states refuses it) brings it to **119**, and requiring an
  invented household to move whole rather than split across the river brings it to **73**.
  That last condition costs 46 moves and is the right price.
- So this ticket spends **73**, not 265, and the town converges to **2,735** — inside the
  model's 2,362-3,265 and 192 above its 2,543 point, against 265 above it today.
  `the_moves_the_rule_yields` in the rule file is the derived list, ready to write into
  the book's ledger.
- What would raise it is stated in the rule file under `what_would_raise_the_ceiling`,
  and none of it is this ticket's: more orders in the women's and children's cells (the
  lodging band), a ruling that an employment seat may be re-seated with the person (the
  staffing band), or a re-family that also re-seats a placed roof (T-1199).

T-1560's report and `docs/LIBERTIES.md` entry should carry these numbers rather than the
265, and should say that the remainder — 450 of the 523 — stays held under T-1459 rather
than clamped.
