Newberry & Dole's store house stands at the Dearborn end of South Water Street by its neighbours' advertisements, and the committed warehouse stands four blocks west at Franklin: rule which building the paper means, then move or keep it.

## FILED OVER THE BUDGET, AND HERE IS THE REASON

The ticket budget refused this: the queue stands at 170 lines, at or over its ceiling of 140. It was filed anyway, on this reason:

> T-1723 is the bank and closes with its PR; this is the block, a geometry move with a bake, a dock and four jaunts behind it, against a corner the owner set on 2026-10-04, so it cannot ride on a records-only PR

**Found by T-1723 (2026-10-05).** T-1723 ruled the BANK: the *Chicago Democrat*'s advertisers put the firm's "store house, on south water street" from 1834 through the scene-date issue (`chicago_democrat_1835_07_01#c001`, Cohen "[…]low Messrs. Newberry and Dole's"). The same advertisers also point at a CORNER, and it is not the committed one:

- W. H. Brown "on Dearborn street, one door from Newberry & Dole's store" (`chicago_democrat_1834_05_28#c004`).
- W. H. Taylor "on Dearborn street, a few rods north of Messrs. Newberry & Dole's Store" (`chicago_democrat_1835_05_20#c020`).

`newberry_dole_warehouse` stands in the Franklin / South Water inside corner (owner, T-2097, L378), about 528 m west of `dole_warehouse_south` (Andreas's Lake-and-Dearborn warehouse, "close to the present site of the Tremont House"). Rule which building the paper means:

- (a) the store house IS `dole_warehouse_south`'s building, and the firm's forwarding house is the same premises: one of the two records is a duplicate;
- (b) a second house on South Water at Dearborn, between Lake-and-Dearborn and the river, with `newberry_dole_warehouse` moving there;
- (c) the Franklin corner stands, with the reason the Dearborn neighbours do not move it written down.

Taylor is "a few rods NORTH of" the store on Dearborn, which fits a store house at Lake-and-Dearborn better than one on South Water's river side; weigh that. The owner set the Franklin corner by eye on 2026-10-04 to get the house out of the road, so a move is shown to him first, in the PR, with the before/after.

**Also on this question, from Bonnell (Andreas vol. 1 pp. 136–137), and folded here rather than filed again:** on 26 August 1835 the firm had a NORTH-bank warehouse at the east end, "adjoining on the west" Hunter & Hinsdale's (which stood "opposite Dr. Kimball's"), with McCormick & Moon's hat store in one part of it and Jesse Butler's tailor shop at the back. T-1723 kept it out of the July scene because nothing dates it before 1 July. Any source that does — a Democrat or American notice for McCormick & Moon, Butler or Hunter & Hinsdale before July 1835 — raises it as a second house of the firm; see docs/RESEARCH/north_bank_east_end_neighbours.md §4.
