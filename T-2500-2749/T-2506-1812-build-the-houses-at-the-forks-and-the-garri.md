## The evidence

- Cooper: "a house a mile to the southeast of the fort, owned by a farmer who supplied the garrison with butter and eggs" (Lee's, T-2487) "and one near the forks of the river occupied by a man named Clark who was a cattle dealer" (Quaife 1913 p. 167).
- Quaife (p. 167): "Whistler's drawing represents two houses at the forks, one occupied by a discharged soldier, and a house and inclosed field north of the river, belonging to Mr. \"Coursoll.\"" The committed trace's 34-item index does not reach them: find them on the sheet (or in Whistler's description, which Quaife also prints), or record that they are not on the sheet the project holds.
- Whistler's description, through Quaife: "Close to the forks on the south side of the main river a small field of eight or nine acres had been reduced to cultivation and made to serve as the company gardens and public cornfield", with a hay meadow along the inner margin of the South Branch timber.

## What to build

The forks houses and Coursoll's house as log dwellings, positions `reconstructed` within "at the forks" / "north of the river" and kept off the 1812 water; Clark may be one of Whistler's two (say whether you merge them and why). The cornfield as ground: a planted field of 8-9 acres by the forks on the south side, its cover bounded by the month and recorded as a liberty. Nothing is placed by a modern street name the sources do not give.

**Acceptance:** the forks houses, Coursoll's house and field, and the company cornfield stand in the 1812 scene with cards citing Quaife's Cooper and Whistler, or a stated reason why one cannot be placed; desktop and 390x780 shots, no page errors.
