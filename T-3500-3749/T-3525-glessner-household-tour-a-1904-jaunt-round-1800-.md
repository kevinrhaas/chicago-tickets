Glessner household tour: a 1904 jaunt round 1800 Prairie told by the people who live and work there.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The owner, 2026-11-08: *"this will be the first jaunt we will show people here i would like you to do a tour of glessner house from the outside by all the people who live there, like have john or frances welcome you and have everyone in the house explain their roles in the house ... we go in and out of this door carrying things...or i am the coachman and this is where we keep the horses ... make it a complete tour of the house and you walk around and visit people ... there is a warm head to the next stop ... make it a real intersting lively story with great facts particular to the era and really help people feel lilke they are there, add this to 1904 jaints"*.

Scope: a new 1904 jaunt, on dev, desktop and a 390x780 phone.

- A new jaunt in the 1904 Jaunts list: a walk all the way round the outside of 1800 Prairie, each stop told in the first person by a member of the household standing there: John or Frances Glessner welcomes the visitor, then the staff the records name (Mattie Williamson, cook; Kate Ryan, cook; Mary Reynolds, house maid; Christine Nelson, parlor maid; Charles Nelson, coachman; August Billings, stable helper) each at their own place (kitchen court, service stair, servants' entry, stable doors, front door), saying who they are, what their work is and what the visitor can see from there; each stop ends handing the visitor on to the next person.
- At each stop that person is in the scene, at their place, turned to the visitor, and greets them (the people a stop calls for come out of the house by its door when they were indoors, and go back about their day when the visitor moves on). Everyone else stays held, as on every jaunt.
- The facts are the house's own research: The Story of a House (1923), HABS, the museum's family and Mattie Williamson pages, the 1900 census. A first-person voice, the words they speak, and anything not in a source (years in service beyond the record, a personal detail) are reconstructed and recorded under a liberty; the gate's quotation and evidence audits pass.
- The jaunt is set in a season the family was at home (not summer, when they were at The Rocks).
- Gate test covers a stop's hosts: placed at their mark facing the stand, greeted, released on leaving.
