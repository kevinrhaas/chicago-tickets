The owner, 2026-11-08: *"when people enter the glessner house from the rear they just kind of disappear at the staircase i think the door should work and the stairs and they should come up and down the stairs"*.

Scope: the 1904 Glessner House (1800 Prairie), on dev, desktop and a 390x780 phone.

- Someone whose day takes them in by the north court service entrance walks to the foot of the service stair, climbs its ten treads to the landing (feet on each tread, no glide or float), crosses to the door, and the door's own baked oak leaf swings in before them; they go through, it shuts behind them, and only then are they indoors. Coming out is the same in reverse: the door opens, they step out on the landing, it shuts, and they walk down the flight to the court.
- The servants' entry off Eighteenth Street (the alcove under the great arch) works the same way: up its front steps and stair to its side door, which opens and shuts.
- The leaf is the photo-matched baked one, lifted from the batch and hung on a pivot as T-3442 does for the front door; behind it a dark vestibule, never daylight through the house. No rebake of the Glessner model.
- Two people passing at once share the door: it stays open until the last one is through.
- doors_1904.json drops its "the climb is not walked" note; the hinge side, swing and vestibule are recorded under a liberty.
- Gate test covers the climb (heights rise tread by tread to the landing) and the door opening before and shutting after a passage.
