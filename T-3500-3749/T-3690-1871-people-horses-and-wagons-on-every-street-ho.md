The 1871 city has its people, horses and wagons on every street, as 1812, 1835 and 1904 do: walkers on the plank walks, drays and delivery wagons, buggies, horse cars on the State Street and Madison Street lines, and for the fire the steam fire engines with their teams and crews, and crowds fleeing with bundles, carts and wagons ahead of the front.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- Everyone is a carded figure through the portable-humans path, dressed for October 1871 (men's sack suits and frock coats, felt hats, workmen's shirts and caps; women's early-bustle day dresses, shawls and bonnets; children), reusing the 1835 and 1904 bodies, faces, garments and gaits with an 1871 wardrobe recorded as reconstructed (LIBERTIES.md) and checked against period photographs.
- Population and traffic densities per district are bounded by the 1870 census (about 299,000 people) and the directory; reconstructed where nothing states them.
- Vehicles reuse the 1904 and 1835 wagons, carriages and horses; the horse car is new from period pictures; the steam fire engine (an Amoskeag-type with its hose cart and team) is new from period pictures of the Chicago Fire Department's engines (the "Little Giant", the "Long John").
- Fire behaviour (driven by T-3687's clock): crowds stream away from the front carrying bundles, pushing carts, dragging trunks; wagons loaded high; engine crews working at hydrants. No one is shown burning in a students' telling; the adults telling may show the dead after the fact, from the accounts.
