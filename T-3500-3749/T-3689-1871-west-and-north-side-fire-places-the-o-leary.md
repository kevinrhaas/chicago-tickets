The West and North Side fire places stand in 1871: the O'Leary cottage and barn at 137 DeKoven Street with their neighbours (the McLaughlins' front house, Dennis Sullivan's view from across the street), the Chicago Avenue Water Tower and its pumping works, the Ogden house at Washington Square (the North Side house that survived), St. Michael's church in Old Town, and the refuges where the North Side fled: the lake shore sands and Lincoln Park with the old City Cemetery.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- Each is a named structure or place in the 1871 scene on its documented site, built to its pictures under the image gate (the 1871 O'Leary cottage photographs and the inquiry's descriptions of the barn and lane; the Water Tower and pumping station prints and photographs; the Ogden house photographs), with a card that says what happened there (cited).
- DeKoven Street's block is a full-detail West Side street of 1871: small frame cottages close together, front and rear houses on narrow lots, sheds, barns, board fences, a dirt street and a plank walk (T-3685), lit by the evening (T-3687 sets the hour).
- The Water Tower survives the fire and the pumping station's roof burns (T-3687); the Ogden house survives; St. Michael's burns to its walls.
- The lake shore and Lincoln Park read as the refuges they were: open sand and graves, room for crowds and their goods (T-3690 fills them).
- Phone keeps them all. Before/after pictures inline in the PR, desktop and phone. Gate green, merged to dev.
