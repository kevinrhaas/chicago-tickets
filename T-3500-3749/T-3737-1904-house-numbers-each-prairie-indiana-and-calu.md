1904 house numbers: each Prairie, Indiana and Calumet house shows its 1904 street number where period houses carried it (gilt figures on the transom, as 1815's photograph shows), one atlas and one draw.

Kevin, 2026-11-23: "i noticed there were no street addresses on most of the buildings could you do a sweep of what the street address numbers/plaque would have been on most buildings and then include them accordingly?" Then: "yes start with 1904, the other years can come later, you can add tickets for the other years for later to the queue, andother thing i am not sure that tree would be in the middle of the sidewalk" (with a screenshot of the east walk before the Pullman house, a parkway elm standing at the edge of the paved crossing).

**What the sources say (read before building):**
- The number: the 1911 Sanborn prints each avenue lot's number (`addresses_1911` in data/street_grid/1904.json). The 1909 renumbering left the district's avenue numbers alone (where.js: Glessner's 1800 is his 1887 address; Pullman's 1729 and Coleman's 1811 are the 1880s sources' numbers; a building permit of 1888 already uses 2110). Cross-street lots were renumbered and get no number.
- Where and how it was shown: 1815's own photograph (`chicagology_1815_sears_two_views`) shows light serif figures "1815" centred on the door's transom glass; the Inter Ocean of 2 January 1887 (`chicagology_1612_prairie_occupants`) describes 1612 "with the number 1612 over the portals"; Glessner's door carries 1800 in its iron grille (sheet 95, already built).

**Acceptance:**
- Every 1904 house with a modelled front door on Prairie, Indiana or Calumet whose lot has a number shows it, read from the street at the door: on the transom glass where the door has one, on the leaf's top rail where it has none. Glessner keeps its grille's 1800 and gets nothing added.
- 1815 and 1612 are marked attested in form; the rest are the number (inferred, from the sheet) in the reconstructed form (a liberty in docs/LIBERTIES.md).
- One texture atlas and one draw call for the whole district; nothing drawn past a short distance; nothing added on a phone's boot beyond a few KB. Desktop 1280x800 and phone 390x780 pictured, zero page errors.
- Before and after pictures inline in the PR and on closing.

**Not in this ticket:** Kevin's tree on the walk (the thread "Move 1904 street trees out of the sidewalk" has it: the pictured Pullman corner elm on the 18th Street crossing); 1871 (filed separately for later), 1835 and 1812 (the town had no house numbers to show; nothing is invented).
