1936 Prairie's materials as its 1869 Tribune describes them: pressed brick, brownstone dressings, porch and bay, rusticated basement.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The owner, 2026-11-22 (project thread "Rebuild 1936 Prairie from its 1880s description"), on 1918 (1936) Prairie in 1904: *"in looking at 1936 prairie it looks like brick and reddish stone and when i read this article it has a very detailed description can you improve this building based on your analysis of this reasearch"* (https://chicagology.com/chicagostreets/prairieavenue/1936prairie/, the Chicago Tribune of 30 October 1869, source `chicago_tribune_1869_10_30_thompson_residence`). Done when the build reads as the Tribune's materials: Baltimore pressed brick on every exterior wall above the basement (the tower included); every window trimmed in brownstone, and the porch, its steps, columns and cornice, and the reception room's half-octagonal bay in stone; the 6 ft Portland brownstone ashlar basement with square rustic joints, a moulded base and a water-table; the slate roof read as slate rather than red tin. Before/after pictures from the street on desktop and phone; the photograph's match record updated and still `matches`; liberties recorded.
