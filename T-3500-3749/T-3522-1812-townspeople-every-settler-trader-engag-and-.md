1812 townspeople: every settler, trader, engagé and woman dressed as their own person, seen at normal distance.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The owner, 2026-11-08, with a screenshot of three 1812 civilian men on the prairie in the same black round hat, dark waistcoat and grey trousers: *"you need to vary the clothing more so everyone is their unique person in 1812 all of these guys wear the same hat and clothing so do that across the board"*.

Scope: every drawn 1812 person who is neither Native (T-3506 did those) nor a soldier (the "Dress 1812 soldiers by rank" thread) nor a child (T-3520): the settlers, traders, engagés, servants, militia men, and the town's women (`settler_1812`, `engage_1812`, `trader_1812`, `woman_working_1812`, `lady_1812`).

- No two of them dressed alike: each person's own cloth colours, pieces worn or left off (waistcoat, neckerchief, sash, apron, kerchief) and headwear, from the 1812 dress research's period range by trade and origin (`docs/RESEARCH/people-1812-dress/`, people-1812-3461 `dress.json`, the 3463..3466 day readings), graded and under a liberty.
- Headwear varies in SHAPE, not only colour (round hats of different crown and brim, straw hats, knit caps in more than one colour, bare heads, the women of the country's head handkerchief), because the silhouette is what reads at a distance.
- The differences read at normal viewing distance (10-30 m) on desktop and on a 390x780 phone, at every LOD; before/after stills of the owner's view.
- No new GLB: recolour clones, hide pieces, small procedural headwear; 1812 phone memory stays under its 350 MB ceiling.
- A generator writes the dress file; the variation persons file carries it; the person card's dress line names what each wears.
