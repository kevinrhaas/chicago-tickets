1812: more trees south of the river: the South Branch timber belt, dune cottonwoods, and willow and cottonwood groves at the wet hollows.

The owner, 2026-11-03 (project thread "Add 1812 worn paths and trees"): *"I also think there should be more trees south of the river it was well documented from before that there were cottonwoods and other occasional groves of trees along the water and in patchy water areas throughout the prairie, the south part should not look so desolate."*

Sources: Andreas via chicagology_prefire273 ("On the South Side, a body of timber grew along the river ... extending south two or three miles"; "a grove up the South Branch"); Currey 1912 ch. I ("a scanty growth of cottonwoods and pines ... among the sand-dunes" from the mouth three or four miles south; the woods near Lee's Place on the South Branch's east side); the flora dossier's PLS reading (Cook County's willow thickets in the sloughs, McBride & Bowles).

**Acceptance:**
- 1812 south of the river reads as prairie with timber: the South Branch belt visibly continuous two to three miles south, scattered dune cottonwoods (and a few pines) along the south shore sand, and small cottonwood / willow / green-ash groves and willow clumps at the wet hollows of the south prairie.
- Species from the zone tables; densities and positions reconstructed, recorded as a liberty; the battle ground's beach and ridge kept open (Heald).
- 1812 phone and desktop memory and tree caps within budget (or a stated re-budget); screenshots before/after.
