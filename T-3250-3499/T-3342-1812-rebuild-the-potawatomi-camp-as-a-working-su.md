1812: rebuild the Potawatomi camp as a working summer camp — varied mat and bark lodges, cooking arbors, hearths, drying racks, storage scaffolds, canoes, a horse ground and trodden earth.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The owner, 2026-11-03 (project thread "Rebuild 1812 Potawatomi camp respectfully"), on a phone screenshot of the camp near today's 20 W Couch Pl: *"I think you can do a much better and respectful job of representing the Potawatomi camp, the texture don't look like anything and this does not look like a camp. Do your research and make reasonable assumptions based on history accounts of normal native camps and how they are organized and what functions buildings etc are there they cannot all be identical geometric domes they must look like natural structures with correct surfaces and the ground on the camps should be correct and the would be I think fires and areas for cooking, etc again do your research and design these camps with a logical and respectful reconstruction"*. Then: *"Yes and any prep areas like where they keep horses and equipment etc"* and *"Anything require for their encampment to exist"*.

Sources read for it: Forsyth 1827 in Blair 1912 v2 pp. 227-28 (summer bark houses, oblong with benches; mat lodges of bent poles under cattail mats, a hanging door, the fire in the centre; spits to roast meat); Wau-Bun 1856 ch. VII (cattail mats sewn with a bone needle, "seldom more than five or six feet in length"; dogs at every lodge), ch. XVI (inside a mat lodge), the Four Lakes "matted lodges" and the bark wigwams of the summer villages; Hubbard 1911 (scaffolds by the lodges carrying mats, bark canoes, mats carried in canoes, Potawatomi ponies); Skinner 1927 (Prairie Potawatomi narratives: "camps and arbors", a "bough arbor"); Currey 1912 chs. II-III (the camping place on the creek, the chief's wigwam, the kettle, the horse at the encampment).

**Acceptance:**
- The three groups stop being identical grey domes: mat lodges of several sizes and shapes, some with bark-sheet roofs, an elongated two-fire lodge, a bark summer house, built of mat panels about 1.6 m long and bark sheets, with real surface relief (a woven-mat and a bark texture, not flat colour).
- The working camp: open hearths with kettles on tripods or cranes and roasting spits, bough-roofed cooking arbors, meat/fish drying racks, raised storage scaffolds with rolled mats and bundles, a hide frame, woodpiles, a wooden mortar, canoes drawn up at the creek mouth, a horse ground with tether stakes and packs.
- Trodden-earth ground laid under each group (yards layer, feathered), clear of T-3339's worn paths.
- Built at the reconstructed tier with sources cited; L-1812-potawatomi-camp-2515 rewritten; `review_required` / `touches_removal` kept with their own words; no ceremony staged.
- 1812 phone memory no worse than dev by more than a few MB; desktop and phone screenshots before/after.
