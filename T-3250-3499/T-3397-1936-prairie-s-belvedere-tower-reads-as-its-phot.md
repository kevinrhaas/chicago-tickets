1936 Prairie's belvedere tower reads as its photograph's, not a flared cap on a plain shaft.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The owner, 2026-11-06, on 1918 (1936) Prairie in 1904: "the fanciful tower just cannot be a thing". The tower itself is attested (the 1869 Tribune enters the house "through a tower", 73 ft to its top; the Chicagology winter photograph draws it), so it stays; what reads as fanciful is the build's top: a 1.1 m slab of pale cornice over a plain shaft with small lights, the skirt above it the cornice's colour. Done when, from the photograph's camera and from above (the owner's 58 ft view), the belvedere reads as the photograph's: its cornice's reach measured on the photograph (about 0.8 m, as the house's own 0.75 m eave), the cap's top a roof in the house's own roofing, and each face's light framed as the photograph's arched opening is; side-by-sides committed; the match record's tower items updated.
