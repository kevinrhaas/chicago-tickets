## What the evidence shows

- HABS sheet 6 (the 1965 photogrammetric front elevation, 75.5 px/ft) draws every Prairie second-floor colonnette: a straight-tapered round shaft about 0.6 ft across under the capital and about 0.78 ft at its foot, standing on a splayed block base about 1.1 ft at the sill and 0.13 of the opening tall; a bell capital about 0.3 of the opening tall and about 1.4 ft across its abacus, carved with leaves and corner volutes, each capital's carving different (notched, beaded and rolled abacus bands).
- The owner's photographs agree: the c. 1888 front (a18-gh-glessner-front-with-keith) shows the tapered shafts and the jointed base block; the 1945 front shows the carved capitals on every group; Taylor 2135 shows the shafts in the same pale granite as the walls. The 1887 construction photograph shows the capitals still uncarved blocks (carved in place later), so the 1904 scene carves them.
- The build: a straight round shaft (no taper), a thin square plinth, the carved capitals only on the group of four over the carved string (the other groups a coarse block), in the TRIM material, which the granite tint at load does not reach.

**Acceptance:** every Prairie colonnette (the groups of three, four and three) built with the sheet-6 taper, the angular splayed base and a carved foliate capital at sheet-6 proportions, varied between colonnettes; dressed in the granite colour (taking the load-time grey-greige tint like the walls); the light tier carries the same silhouette; Glessner rebaked; desktop and phone before/after beside the owner's photographs shown to him.
