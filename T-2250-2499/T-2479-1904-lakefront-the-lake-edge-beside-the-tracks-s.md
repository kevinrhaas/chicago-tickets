1904 lakefront: the lake edge beside the tracks, stone riprap on the embankment face with its timber cap, and the made-ground strip between the last track and the water.

Owner, 2026-10-13 (project thread "1904 lakefront tracks and shore"), the same ask as T-2478: check whether there was a beach like 1835's, what the 1904 shoreline held, and build it photographic.

## What the sources say (read 2026-10-13)

- No beach. The Greeley-Carlson atlas of 1891 draws the water's edge a few metres east of the tracks and the Illinois Central breakwater (1852 on: 12x12 timber cribs filled with stone, held by piles at 10-ft intervals, chicagoarchitecturehistory.com 8.6) offshore. The 1911 Sanborn sheets draw the edge 14-21 m further east (made ground, L287) as a straight built line, no sand, no walk. The lakefront park and its beaches came with the Burnham Park fill of 1919-1930s.
- Sanborn 1911 sheet 28 puts about 16 m of made ground between the easternmost track and the water at 18th Street.

**Acceptance:** from the lake looking west and from the foot of 18th Street looking along the shore, the embankment face shows rough stone riprap down to and under the water with a timber cap along its crest, and the strip between the last track and the edge reads as made ground (cinders, sand and weeds), not lawn; desktop and 390x780; phone memory and frame cost measured and kept in budget; reconstructions recorded as liberties.
