1904 lakefront: the Illinois Central and Michigan Central tracks east of Prairie Avenue, rails, ties and cinder ballast on the 1911 track layout, with weedy verges.

Owner, 2026-10-13 (project thread "1904 lakefront tracks and shore"): *"there would be train tracks and some kind of flora around that and what the land would have been for the train lines under that, make those correct textures ... check me on all of that ... build photographic quality materials and items so the images of that shore line are photographic in quality when done"*.

## What the sources say (read 2026-10-13)

- **Sanborn 1911 vol. 3 sheets 20 and 28** (georeferenced, data/traces/gcp/) draw every track as two rails: 11 tracks at 18th Street, about 15 at 16th, on the Illinois Central / Michigan Central right-of-way, with the lake against its east side. That is the layout the scene's waterline (L287) already stands on.
- **Greeley-Carlson atlas 1891** (Prairie library `st-greeley-carlson-1891-prairie-environs`) draws 4-5 tracks, the water's edge just east of them and the 1850s breakwater offshore: the right-of-way widened between 1891 and 1911.
- **George Glessner's photographs** (`st-ghm-george-glessner-ic-train-18th`, looking north from 18th St, c. 1887-1900; `a18-gh-ic-train-foot-of-18th-c1888`, looking east): a wide multi-track yard at street grade, dark cinder ballast filled nearly to the tie tops, ties exposed, bare trodden ground between tracks, no lawn, no crushed white stone.

**Acceptance:** standing at the foot of 18th Street and on the right-of-way looking north (the Glessner photograph's view), the 1904 scene shows the traced tracks as rails on ties on a dark cinder bed across the whole right-of-way, with weedy grass along its edges instead of the lawn the terrain draws there today; desktop and 390x780 both; the track layout is generated from the sheets by a command; every reconstructed part (1904 track count, sections, tie spacing, ballast, weeds) is a liberty.
