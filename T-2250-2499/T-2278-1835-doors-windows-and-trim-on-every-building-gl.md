1835 doors, windows and trim on every building: Glessner dark glass in glazed sash, panelled and batten doors, casings, sills and drip caps.

Owner, 2026-10-10 (project thread): give every 1835 structure correct door and window openings and appropriate trim/fascia; check the history of glass; reuse Glessner's dark glass wherever a building could reasonably have had glass; otherwise an opening that renders well and looks photographic.

History, from sources already held: window glass (8x10, 7x9, 5x10) and ready-made window sash were advertised by at least five Chicago merchants in the Chicago Democrat 1833-35 (biz_philo_carpenter, biz_john_h_kinzie, biz_j_k_botsford, biz_p_f_peck, biz_jones_king). So every dwelling, store, tavern and fort building may be glazed; barns, sheds and stables stay unglazed.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

1. Every glazed pane in the 1835 town draws as Glessner's dark pane (the T-2109/T-2183 `dark` glass: ~0.11 grey, roughness 0.065), at no extra draw call.
2. No 1835 door is a black void: houses, taverns and stores get closed panelled doors (stores half-glazed), cabins and outbuildings board-and-batten doors.
3. Windows on frame dwellings, log dwellings, storefronts, taverns and fort buildings stand in a casing proud of the wall with a sill and drip cap, the sash set back in it.
4. Before/after shots at both viewports; triangle cost measured and any ceiling move stated; check.sh green; LIBERTIES entry.
